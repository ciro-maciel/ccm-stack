# Start from the person

A piece works when the person who meets it recognizes their own situation, gets something useful before being asked for anything, and leaves knowing the next step. Write for one real reader, not for "users".

## Before you write

Answer these five in a few lines each. If you cannot answer one, find out (read the code, the ticket, the analytics, the support thread) before writing.

1. **Who arrives, and in what state?** In a hurry, worried, curious, comparing options, coming from an error?
2. **What do they want and not say?** The need under the request: to not look foolish, to save the afternoon, to be sure nothing breaks.
3. **What blocks them?** Doubt, jargon, a missing fact, a step that looks risky, a price they cannot find.
4. **What do they leave with?** One concrete result: a working setup, an answer, a decision.
5. **What is the next step?** One action, named plainly.

## While you write

- **Name their tension** in their words, so they recognize themselves in the first lines.
- **Prove by use.** Show the thing working (a command, a screenshot, an example with real values) instead of describing how good it is.
- **Give before you ask.** The useful part comes before the sign-up, the star, the purchase.
- **True facts only.** No invented scarcity, deadlines, numbers, testimonials or "most people". No guilt for whoever says no.
- **One next step**, never three competing ones.

## Review from their side

Reread the draft as that person, arriving in that state.

| Check | Fails when |
|---|---|
| Recognition | The first lines talk about you, your product or your process |
| Value first | They must act or pay before getting anything useful |
| Proof | A claim has no example, number or demonstration behind it |
| Honesty | Any number, quote or urgency cannot be traced to a real source |
| Next step | There is no clear action, or there are several |
| Language | A word they would not use, an internal name, a code name |

Fix every failing row, then say in one line which rows you fixed.
